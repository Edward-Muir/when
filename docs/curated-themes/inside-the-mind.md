# The Inside the Mind theme

A 30-card curated theme on the study of the mind and brain, from the Edwin Smith Papyrus to
optogenetics. This note is why these 30 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is a landmark of psychology, psychiatry or brain science.**

- **Psychiatric drugs are in.** Lithium, chlorpromazine and Prozac belong here. Other medicines
  go to Wonder Drugs, scanners to Seeing Inside, and surgery to Under the Knife.
- **Treatments are in, including the condemned ones.** The first lobotomy and the first
  electroconvulsive therapy are landmarks of psychiatry whatever came after.
- **The blind spine had 42 beats, and 12 were cut** for fame, crowding or the bunching limit
  (no more than 3 beats in any 10 years). The cuts include Valencia's asylum (1409), Burton's
  Anatomy of Melancholy (1621), Willis's brain anatomy (1664), the York Retreat (1796), Dix
  (1843), Golgi (1873), Wernicke (1874), Kraepelin (1883), Alzheimer (1906), DSM-III (1980),
  mirror neurons (1996) and the BRAIN Initiative (2013).

Edge arguably worth a look: `edwin-smith-surgical-papyrus` is a surgical text that is also the
first description of the brain. It is shared with `under-the-knife` and `seeing-inside`, one
card each, well under the limit of four.

## Known crowding kept on purpose

Twelve pairs sit inside the 8-year advisory window. The tight runs are the 1920/1921 pair
(Little Albert, Rorschach), 1952/1953 (chlorpromazine, H.M.) and 1969-1973 (Kubler-Ross,
Stanford Prison, Rosenhan). They are the real shape of mid-century psychology, and each card is
too well known to cut. The 300 BCE to 1000 stretch is empty because the spine found no landmark
there that was not a medical text already owned by another theme.

## Fact-check flags still unsupported

- **`bethlem-hospital-first-patients` (1247):** 1247 is the founding of the priory, which served
  pilgrims. The mentally ill are first documented there in 1403. The year contradicts the name
  and description, so this card needs a decision from Edward.
- **`gall-founds-phrenology` (1810):** sources split between about 1796, when Gall began
  lecturing, and 1810, when he published.
- **`prozac-reaches-market` (1987):** the FDA approved it in December 1987, but Lilly launched it
  in early 1988.

The spine writer searched only 14 of its 42 dates, and the fact-checker searched only some of
the rest, so the other years rest on the agents' own knowledge. The detail-prose writer wrote
the Stanford Prison, Prozac and Moniz entries from memory and suggests checking those first.
