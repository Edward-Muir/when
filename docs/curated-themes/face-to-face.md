# The Face to Face theme

A 30-card curated theme on rulers meeting rulers and envoys standing before foreign courts, from
a Han envoy riding west to Reagan and Gorbachev at Reykjavik. This note is why these 30 and not
others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is a summit of rulers or an embassy to a foreign court.**

- **The summit**: heads of state, or the people acting for them, meeting in person. Congresses
  of powers (`congress-vienna`, `congress-of-berlin`) count, as do wartime conferences of
  leaders (`tehran-conference`, `yalta-conference`).
- **The embassy**: an envoy, friar or delegation sent by one ruler and received by another.
  The card is the meeting or the mission, not the trade or the faith that followed it.

The boundary with the neighbouring themes:

- **Peace at Last** takes the war-ending treaties, Tilsit included. A congress that redraws a
  map after a war (Vienna, Berlin) stays here because the card is the gathering, not a peace
  signed between belligerents.
- **Iron Curtain** takes the Cold War crises. The Cold War summits here (Geneva 1955, Vienna
  1961, Reykjavik 1986) are meetings, not crises.
- **Silk Road** takes merchants and pilgrims on the caravan roads. `zhang-qian-western-mission`
  sits on that edge and is in because Zhang Qian was a ruler's envoy seeking an alliance, not a
  trader.

Edges argued rather than assumed:

- **`congress-vienna`** is reused at the catalogue's 1815, the final act. The spine had the
  1814 opening; it is the same congress under a different date convention.
- **`vasco-da-gama-india`** is reused for da Gama's reception by the Zamorin at Calicut: the
  arrival and the audience are one event.
- **`peter-great-grand-embassy`** is written new, because the existing `peter-great-modernizes`
  is about the reforms that followed, not the embassy itself.

17 of the 30 were written for this theme.

Gates: size **30**, band 0 **22**, bins **7/8** (advisory), same-year pairs **0**, bunching
**3 in 1938-1945** (limit 3), shared **1** (chinese-dynasties). A card's band moves whenever the
catalogue around its year does, so re-measure rather than trust these figures.

Band 0 is high because the blind grader found nearly every new card's era easy to read from its
text: a caliph, a Mongol khan, a samurai, Louis XIV and a tsar each date their card. The
placement-first grid makes those easy, however obscure the envoy.

## Deliberate omissions

Ten spine beats were cut to reach 30, the thinner ones on fame or the ones crowding a
stretch: Pope Stephen crossing to Pepin (754), Olga of Kiev at Constantinople (957), Manuel II
in London (1400, four years from Clavijo), Chancellor at the Muscovite court (1553), the
Tensho boy envoys in Rome (1585), the Four Mohawk Kings (1710), Amherst's embassy (1816, one
year from Vienna), Edward VII in Paris (1903, two years from Bjorko), Khrushchev's American
tour (1959) and the Malta summit (1989). The Atlantic Charter meeting (1941) was dropped from
the spine to keep 1938-1945 to three cards.

## Known crowding

theme-gap flags six pairs inside 8 years. 1519/1520 (`cortes-meets-aztecs` → `field-of-cloth-of-gold`)
are on different continents, and the first is a foothold. 1938/1943/1945 is the honest shape of
wartime summitry, held to the three-card limit.

## Fact check

All 17 new cards came back `ok`, with no fixes and nothing unsupported. The checker searched
only three of them (Zhang Qian, Hasekura and Ibn Fadlan) and confirmed the rest from memory and
the spine's sources; the spine writer also skipped its per-date searches. The years are the
standard ones, but they are worth a glance in review. Ibn Fadlan's card takes the departure
year (921; he reached the Volga in 922), and Zhang Qian's takes 138 BCE (a minority gives 139).

Raised by the detail writer:

- **`matteo-ricci-enters-beijing`**: most sources say Ricci never met the Wanli emperor in
  person. The description's "an audience at the Ming court" is defensible (his gifts were
  received at court), but it could read as an imperial audience. The detail prose leads with
  the correction.
- The detail prose for Cleopatra, Peter the Great, Vienna 1961, Sadat and Reykjavik was
  written from memory rather than searched, and is worth a spot-check.
