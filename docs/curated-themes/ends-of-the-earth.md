# The Ends of the Earth theme

A 36-card curated theme on the first human reach of a planetary extreme — a pole, a great
summit, an unvisited landmass, an icecap or desert crossed for the first time. This note is
why these 36 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if it is **the first time people reached a pole, a great summit, or an
unvisited extreme of the planet**: a deepest point, a remote landmass or island first settled,
or a crossing of an icecap or desert. Voyages that merely charted a coast are out — that is the
existing Mapmakers theme's territory, not this one.

Two edges were argued rather than assumed:

- **`cook-crosses-antarctic-circle`** (spine, 1773) is left out. Cook's expedition did not land
  anywhere; it sailed south of a latitude line and turned back. Read against the exclusion rule
  as written — "voyages that merely charted a coast are out" — a ship passing a parallel without
  putting a foot down is structurally the same act as charting, just further south. The genuine
  firsts of Antarctic contact are `first-landing-on-antarctica` (1895) and
  `belgica-overwinters-antarctica` (1898), both kept.
- **The four different ways of reaching the North Pole** — Peary's disputed 1909 claim
  (`north-pole-reached`), the 1926 airship overflight, Plaisted's 1968 snowmobile party, and the
  1990 unsupported ski trek — read at first like redundancy. They are kept as three (the airship
  is cut below) because each is a genuinely separate "first": first claimed, first confirmed
  surface party, first unsupported. That progression is one of the theme's better throughlines,
  not padding.

## Why the footholds are the footholds

Band 0 blends the `difficulty` label with how sparse the timeline is around a year. This theme
has exactly the required floor of 5, not a comfortable margin, and that is structural rather
than a grading choice:

- `humans-reach-australia` and `humans-reach-the-americas` sit in the emptiest part of the whole
  catalogue — nothing else competes for those millennia, so both are trivially ordered even
  though a player could not date either to within ten thousand years.
- `vikings-iceland` and `viking-america` are genuinely famous (Norse settlement, Leif Erikson)
  and both already carry an `easy`/`medium` label with little else nearby.
- `south-pole-reached` (Amundsen) is the one modern foothold: `easy`-labelled and one of the
  handful of polar facts everyone holds.

Everything else in the theme's back half (1888-1997) sits in band 1-3, because that stretch is
exactly where the catalogue is densest with expedition history — the theme's own subject matter
crowds it. `everest` itself lands band 2 for that reason, not because the summit isn't famous.

## Deliberate omissions

- **Out of scope.** `cook-crosses-antarctic-circle` (spine beat, 1773) — argued above; it is a
  charting voyage in substance, and stays out.
- **Crowding, cut in favour of the stronger version of a nearby beat.** The spine's full run of
  eight-thousand-meter first ascents (Annapurna 1950, Everest 1953, K2 1954, Kangchenjunga
  1955, Manaslu 1956) and the later batch (Puncak Jaya 1962, Shishapangma 1964) packs fourteen
  years with nine near-identical "first ascent of an 8,000m peak" beats. Kept: Annapurna (the
  first ever climbed), Everest (the highest, and an existing catalogue card), and K2 (the
  second-highest and most dangerous). Cut: `kangchenjunga`, `manaslu`, `shishapangma` and
  `puncak-jaya` — each a real first, but a fourth, fifth, sixth and seventh instance of the same
  beat is redundancy a 36-card deck cannot afford once three are already in.
- **Crowding, cut for lower narrative weight.** The 1926 Norge airship flight over the North
  Pole was cut from the four-ways-to-the-pole set (see above) as the least distinct of the four
  — "first flight" reads closer to Byrd's 1929 South Pole flight, which is kept because it has
  no competing polar-flight card on its own side of the planet.
- **Spine beats that were already the same beat as a kept card, at a different date.** None
  here; every remaining spine beat maps to exactly one slug.

## Still missing from the catalogue

Real gaps, not written because they would collide with a card already in the 36:

- **Robert Falcon Scott's race to the South Pole** (1912, arriving five weeks after Amundsen) —
  a second-place finish, not a first, so it fails the scope rule outright rather than merely
  crowding.
- **Hillary and Norgay's route-finding on the Western Cwm and Khumbu Icefall** — folds into
  `everest`, the summit card already in the deck.
- **The Seven Summits challenge being completed** (Messner and others, mid-1980s) — a compound
  achievement built from ascents already individually covered by cards in this theme, not a new
  extreme in its own right.
- **Amelia Earhart's transatlantic and Pacific flights** — genuine aviation firsts, but distance
  records rather than extremes of place, so out of scope.
- **The Kangchenjunga, Manaslu, Shishapangma and Puncak Jaya first ascents** — real, in-scope
  gaps, cut above for crowding rather than merit. Any future edit that opens room in the
  1950-1966 stretch should reach for these first.
