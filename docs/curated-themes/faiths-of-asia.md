# The Faiths of Asia theme

A 30-card curated theme on the milestones of Asia's religions, from the Vedic hymns to the
destruction of the Bamiyan Buddhas: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Taoism, Confucianism, Shinto
and Sikhism. This note is why these 30 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is a milestone of Buddhism, Hinduism or another Asian faith**: a
founder's life, a conversion, a scripture set down, a school founded, a faith carried to a new
land, or a blow struck against one.

The boundary with the neighbouring themes:

- **Temples as buildings go to Houses of Worship.** A card is in here for what happened to the
  faith, not for the architecture. `tsongkhapa-founds-ganden` is in as the founding of the
  Gelug school, whose seat it was; `bamiyan-buddhas-destroyed` is in as an assault on a faith's
  heritage.
- **Islam goes to The Caliphs**, and the Diamond Sutra to Letters & Type.
- **Sikhism is included**, as an Asian faith with its own founding.

Two edges were argued rather than assumed:

- **`nara-todaiji`** is reused for the spine's "Great Buddha of Nara consecrated" beat. The
  existing card is titled for the temple's completion, but its description is about the giant
  Buddha and the year is the same. It leans toward Houses of Worship; swap in a new
  consecration card if that boundary should be strict.
- **`beatles-study-meditation-india`** is in as the moment Asian meditation reached a mass
  Western audience, the same arc as `vivekananda-chicago-address` and
  `hare-krishna-movement-founded`.

## Deliberate omissions

Cut from the spine as fame-1 beats that crowded the deck past 30: the First Buddhist Council,
Buddhism reaching Sri Lanka, the Han court adopting Confucian scholars, Faxian's journey,
Kumarajiva in Chang'an, Samye, Kukai bringing Shingon to Japan, the 845 suppression of
Buddhism in China, Kublai Khan honouring a Tibetan lama, the Brahmo Samaj, the Arya Samaj, Cao
Dai, Soka Gakkai and the Sixth Buddhist Council. Several of them would fill the empty
1715-1856 stretch only if a later beat moved; none currently falls inside it.

## Known crowding

Theme-gap flags 1191/1193 (Zen reaches Japan, Nalanda sacked), 1956/1959 (Ambedkar, the Dalai
Lama's flight) and 1966/1968 (Hare Krishna, the Beatles in Rishikesh). Each pair is in
different countries and different faiths, so they are kept.

## Fact-check flags

- **`tao-te-ching-written` (-400)** is unsupported: sources give the 4th century BCE, or the
  late 4th to early 3rd, and none names -400. The evidence-window pass rejected a window
  (uncertainty about one composition, not a span of writing), so -400 stands as a placeholder.
- Traditional or debated dates kept as written: Mahavira's -599 (the Jain tradition; scholars
  put him 50-100 years later), Ashoka's -261 (the Kalinga war; when he converted is debated)
  and Adi Shankara's 788 (some scholars argue c. 700).
- The spine's years were mostly written from memory, and the fact checker searched only the
  disputed ones; the later cards (Kojiki, Nichiren, Ganden, the Dalai Lama title, the Guru
  Granth Sahib, the 1868 order, Hare Krishna) were marked ok without a search.
