# The Take to the Streets theme

A 30-card curated theme on famous protest marches and demonstrations, from the Boston Tea Party
to the 2022 protests in Iran. This note is why these 30 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is a famous protest march or demonstration**: people gathering in
public, on foot, to be seen and heard.

The neighbours keep their own:

- strikes go to Down Tools;
- racial-equality protests go to Civil Rights Road;
- Gandhi's Salt March goes to The Raj;
- armed uprisings go to Rise Up!

Edges argued rather than assumed:

- **`bloody-sunday-st-petersburg`** (1905) is in as a petition march fired upon, not as the
  start of a revolution. It is a reused card, shared with the Romanovs theme.
- **`petrograd-womens-day-protest`** (1917) is in as the street protest itself, not the
  revolution it grew into.
- **`stonewall-riots`** is in as a famous street demonstration against a raid.
- **`boston-tea-party`** is in as a political protest, not an armed act; it is shared with
  What We Drink.

The spine ran to 38 beats. Eight were cut, first the obscure British reform meetings (Spa
Fields, Newhall Hill, Hyde Park 1855 and 1866, the 1884 franchise demonstration), then the
Stamp Act protests (the existing `stamp-act` card is the law, not the protests), the Women's
Sunday rally and the Moscow White House defence.

## Gates

Size 30, band 0 is 16 (needs 5), no same-year pairs, at most 3 in any 10 years (1913-1922),
and at most 1 card shared with any one theme (`what-we-drink`: `boston-tea-party`; `romanovs`:
`bloody-sunday-st-petersburg`). Spread is 4 of 8 bins, which is advisory: nothing before 1773.

## Fact-check flags

None are `unsupported`. Wording the checker judged acceptable but loose: the First National
Hunger March's stated demands rest on general knowledge, and "coordinated" for the 2003 Iraq
War protests is not in the sources.
