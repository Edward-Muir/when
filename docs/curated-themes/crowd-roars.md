# The Crowd Roars theme

A 30-card curated theme on famous live music: a dated concert, tour or music festival, from a
Welsh court contest in 1176 to a stadium tour in 2023. This note is why these 30 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is a famous, dated live performance, tour or music festival.**

- **The performance itself**: a premiere, a landmark recital, a benefit show, a festival's
  first edition or its most talked-about set.
- **Tours** are admitted when the tour is the story (Jenny Lind in America, the Eras Tour),
  and the card is dated to the start.

Edges, as the reconciler argued them:

- Carnivals and parades belong to Let's Celebrate, and opera and theatre premieres to On Stage.
  Concert premieres of orchestral and choral works stay here because the occasion is the
  audience and the hall, not a staged drama.
- Concert series (Haydn's London season, the Proms as a run) were cut for the single opening
  night: `first-promenade-concert` stays as the one dated start.
- Fame-1 beats in crowded stretches were cut: the Royal Fireworks rehearsal, Mendelssohn's Bach
  revival, Paganini's London debut, Boston's Peace Jubilee, Ellington at Carnegie Hall and the
  first Newport Folk Festival.

## Gates

size 30, band 0 of 21 (the grader rated most new cards easily recognisable, so the deck leans
easy), no same-year pairs, bunching 3 in 1965-1970, largest overlap 3 with `great-composers`
(`handel-messiah`, `beethoven-ninth`, `rhapsody-in-blue-premiere`). The advisory spread is 6 of
8 bins; nothing before 1176 is in scope.

## Fact-check flags

None are `unsupported`. The fact-check agent ran no searches and rested on the spine's sources;
the spine's dates were searched once each. The one `fix` (a Live 8 description that hinted at
the year) is applied. The detail writer's list of dates resting on its own knowledge (One World
18 April 2020, Mercury's death in November 1991, the Carnegie Hall naming, the Hollywood Bowl
association's founding in October 1920) is worth a glance in review.
