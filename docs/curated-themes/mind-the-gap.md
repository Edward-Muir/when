# The Mind the Gap theme

A 30-card curated theme on the opening of city transit systems, from the first scheduled coaches
in Paris to the metros and busways of the 2020s. This note is why these 30 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is the opening of a city metro, tram or bus system.**

- **The opening itself**: the first service of a metro, tramway, cable car, omnibus or bus
  rapid transit line in a city. A first line standing for a whole system (Paris, Moscow, Delhi)
  counts the same as a network that opened in one go.
- **Edges argued rather than assumed**:
  - `curitiba-bus-rapid-transit` and `bogota-transmilenio-opens` are in. A dedicated busway
    carrying a whole city's trips is a transit system, and both open as one.
  - `crossrail-elizabeth-line` is in as a city metro-style line: a cross-city railway through
    deep central tunnels, opened to passengers in 2022.
  - `bus-service` (the Paris five-sol coaches) is in as the first scheduled urban transport.
- **Excluded**: intercity railways (All Aboard!), tunnels as engineering feats (Bridges &
  Tunnels), and bicycles and cars (Pedal & Petrol). Four fame-1 openings (Swansea and Mumbles,
  Birkenhead, Barcelona, Osaka) were cut from crowded or sparse stretches.

## Gates

theme-gap: size 30, band 0 is 5, no same-year pairs, at most 3 in any 10 years (3 in
1881-1890), and 1 card shared with another theme (`london-underground`, with `english-history`).
Spread covers 5 of 8 bins, which is advisory, and many neighbours sit within a few years of each
other, so placement leans on the era cues in each card.

## Fact-check flags

Years were confirmed for all 26 checked cards. Three descriptions were marked unsupported:

- `curitiba-bus-rapid-transit`: says the system launched with raised tube stations, which came
  around 1991-92.
- `shanghai-metro-opens`: "longest metro network in the world" is contested; current sources
  rank it second by route length.
- `rome-metro-opens`: the "ancient ruins" claim was replaced, since sources blame WWII for the
  slow build of Line B.

Only the first two remain in the text to review; Rome was reworded and its difficulty set to
medium because naming Rome places its era.
