# The Iron Curtain theme

A 30-card curated theme on the Cold War's crises and proxy wars, from Churchill's 1946 speech to
the Soviet Union's dissolution in 1991. 23 cards were already in the catalogue and 7 are new.
This note is why these 30 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is a Cold War crisis or proxy war**: a confrontation between the
blocs, or a war fought in their shadow.

Neighbours take what is theirs: bombs and arms treaties go to The Atomic Age, alliances to Safety
in Numbers, communism's own revolutions to Red Flags, spies to Spy Games.

Six beats from the spine were cut, each the weakest on scope or the least famous in a crowded
decade:

- **MacArthur fired (1951)**: weaker than the war itself.
- **Austrian State Treaty (1955)**: a neutrality settlement, not a crisis.
- **Eisenhower Doctrine (1957)**: Middle East policy, not a crisis.
- **Khrushchev's Berlin ultimatum (1958)**: Berlin is already covered by the airlift and the Wall.
- **Six-Day War (1967)**: a regional war, only loosely a proxy conflict.
- **Sino-Soviet clash (1969)**: communist rivalry, which belongs to Red Flags.

## Gates

theme-gap: size 30, band 0 is 13, no same-year pairs, at most 8 cards in any 10 years, and the
largest overlap with another theme is 2 (the walls: `berlin-wall-built`, `berlin-wall-fall`).

## Fact-check flags

None are `unsupported`, but the fact-check and the spine ran without web searches, so the seven
new cards were checked from knowledge only. The Poland martial law description says the
government "banned the independent union"; Solidarity was suspended in December 1981 and formally
banned in 1982. The region audit also notes that `ogaden-war-somalia-ethiopia` names the Soviet
Union and Cuba and `reagan-tear-down-this-wall` names the Soviet Union, none of which are tagged.
