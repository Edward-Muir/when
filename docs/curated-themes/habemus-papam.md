# The Habemus Papam theme

A 30-card curated theme on the papacy, from the Council of Nicaea to the election of the first
American pope: elections, councils, schisms, treaties and the pontiffs themselves. This note is
why these 30 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is a moment in the history of the papacy**: a pope elected,
resigning, crowning or condemning, a council a pope called or that decided who is pope, or an act
that changed what the papacy is.

Edges argued rather than assumed:

- **`council-nicaea`** is in. It predates the papacy as an institution of power, but it is the
  council every later one answers to, and religious beginnings otherwise go to Faith Founded.
- **`english-reformation-henry`** is in as the break with Rome from the pope's side; the Crusades
  and Kings of France own the neighbouring cases.
- **`napoleon-coronation`** stands for the spine's "Pius VII at the coronation": the same event in
  the same year, reused rather than rewritten.
- **`year-three-popes`** stands for the 1978 election of the first Polish pope, because its
  description covers John Paul II's election.
- The Fourth Lateran Council is new: `medieval-marriage-reform` is the same year but names only
  the marriage-law outcome.

The spine had 41 beats; 11 were cut for density in crowded decades or lower fame (754, 1059,
1268, 1300, 1521, 1798, 1891, 1950, 1979, 1992, 2000).

## Gates

size 30 · band 0 12 (want 5+) · same-year pairs 0 · bunching 2 in 1506-1508 (max 3) · largest
overlap 2 (rome: council-nicaea, great-schism). Two near pairs, 1506/1508 and 1978/1981, sit
under the limit.

## Fact-check flags

None `unsupported`. One fix applied: the 1492 description said "Renaissance pope", a dating word,
now "notorious pope". Detail prose for the newest cards (Pius IX, Benedict XVI, Francis, Leo XIV)
had some claims written from memory, so spot-check them.

## Intro

The popup this theme opens with (rules in [index.md](index.md#theme-intros)).

- **Blurb:** Popes, councils and the Vatican, including rival claimants, abdications and elections that reshaped the Church.
- **Fact:** The word conclave comes from Latin roots meaning together and key, because the cardinals who elect a pope are locked in a room.

Sources: [wordorigins.org](https://www.wordorigins.org/big-list-entries/conclave) (comes from either, or both, the French conclave and the Latin conclave, a locked room or chamber; con- (together) + -clavis (key)).
