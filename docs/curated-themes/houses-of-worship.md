# The Houses of Worship theme

A 30-card curated theme on famous sacred buildings, from the Middle Kingdom temples of Egypt to
the reopening of Notre-Dame: temples, churches, cathedrals, mosques and shrines. This note is why
these 30 and not others.

## The scope rule

An event is in only if **it is the building of a famous temple, church, mosque or shrine**: its
founding, completion, dedication or consecration, or the rebuilding that made it what it is.

Edges handed to neighbouring themes, per the theme boundary:

- Spires and towers that set height records go to Tallest in the World.
- Angkor Wat goes to Temple Kingdoms, the Golden Temple to Faiths of Asia, the Dome of the Rock
  to The Caliphs, Hagia Sophia to The Roman Story.

Two edges were argued rather than assumed:

- **`notre-dame-paris-reopens`** is in. A reopening after a fire is a rebuilding, the same shape
  as `notre-dame-construction` bookending the theme's most famous building.
- **`hassan-ii-mosque-inaugurated`** is in despite its minaret. The card is about the mosque, and
  its description claims only "one of the tallest", not a record.

Spine beats cut to reach 30 (the weaker or more crowded of the 40): Ephesus, the Second Temple,
Ise, Damascus, Santiago, Milan, Kinkaku-ji, the Temple of Heaven, Selimiye and the Lotus Temple.

14 reused cards and 16 new ones. Existing cards that were close but not the same event, so a new

Gates: size **30**, band 0 **14** (after the blind grade), bins **7/8** (advisory), same-year
pairs **0**, bunching **2** in 1194-1200, most shared with any one theme **1**. A card's band
moves whenever the catalogue around its year does, so re-measure rather than trust these figures.

## Known crowding and gaps

- **1194 / 1200** (`gothic-cathedral-construction`, `ethiopian-lalibela-churches`) and **1955 /
  1962** (`notre-dame-du-haut`, `coventry-cathedral-consecrated`) sit inside the 8-year advisory
  window. Both pairs are different buildings on different continents or eras of style, so the
  placement is less luck than the window suggests.
- The timeline has nothing between 1746 and 1863. No famous house of worship in the spine falls
  in that stretch.

## Fact check

Two descriptions were corrected (Djenné's river, Hassan II's minaret claim). Every other year and
claim held up against a search. Notes the checker left that need no change:

- Abu Simbel's start is commonly given as 1264 BC, with some sources allowing 1244 BC.
- Córdoba is given as 785 on Wikipedia, 786 or 784/5 elsewhere.
- St Basil's is given as 1561, and about 1560 in some references.
- St Paul's was finished in 1710, and Parliament declared it so in 1711.

The spine's years were written from memory, not searched; the fact check is the first source
check they got. Nothing is still `unsupported`.

## Intro

The popup this theme opens with (rules in [index.md](index.md#theme-intros)).

- **Blurb:** Thirty temples, cathedrals, mosques and shrines, each dated to the start or completion of its building.
- **Fact:** The Great Hypostyle Hall at Karnak had a roof held up by 134 giant columns, in a hall about 102 metres long and 53 metres wide.

Sources: [guinnessworldrecords.com](<https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/largest-hypostyle-(pillared)-hall>) (had a roof supported by 134 giant columns and is 102 m (335 ft) long and 53 m (174 m) wide).
