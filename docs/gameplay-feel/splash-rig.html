Why today's ripple does not read as a splash

    
    
      
  • 
        The wave moves one row every 200 ms, so the third row out starts moving 600 ms after the
        card lands. A splash front is several times faster.
      
    • 
      
  • 
        Every row is pushed down, including the rows above the card. Water moves away from the
        impact: rows above should lift and rows below should sink.
      
    • 
      
  • 
        The card itself slides up into place and never dips, so nothing appears to cause the wave.
      
    • 
      
  • 
        Each row then rings on its own soft spring for about two seconds, which reads as jelly.
      
    • 
    

    

      The rig shows the board motion only. Confetti, the screen-edge pulse and haptics are
      unchanged and are left out here so the wave is easy to judge.
